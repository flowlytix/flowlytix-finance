How much tax will you pay on a ฿50,000, ฿80,000 or ฿100,000 salary in Thailand?

Know your take-home pay before payday, and planning the whole year gets much easier.

Flowlytix Money · 1 November 2026 · 2026 tax year rules

Your salary is not the money you can spend

When we talk about salary, we usually think of the number in our employment contract. But what lands in your bank account is that salary minus social security and the tax your employer withholds. If you plan your spending around the full number, you keep running short at the end of the month without knowing why.

How Thai salary tax works (the short version)

  1. Add up your income for the year (monthly salary × 12 + bonus).
  2. Take off expenses: 50% of that income, up to ฿100,000.
  3. Take off your allowances, such as the ฿60,000 personal allowance and the social security you paid.
  4. Work out the tax on what is left, step by step: 0% / 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% / 25% / 30% / 35%.

Example: how much tax on your salary?

With only the personal allowance and social security, and no bonus:

When the salary doubles from ฿50,000 to ฿100,000, the tax grows almost six times. The extra pay falls into higher tax steps. That is why a raise often feels smaller than you expected.

ภาษีเงินเดือนคิดอย่างไร (แบบสั้นที่สุด)

  1. นำเงินได้ทั้งปีมาตั้ง (เงินเดือน × 12 + โบนัส)
  2. หักค่าใช้จ่าย 50% แต่ไม่เกิน 100,000 บาท
  3. หักค่าลดหย่อน เช่น ส่วนตัว 60,000 บาท และประกันสังคมที่จ่ายไป
  4. ได้เงินได้สุทธิ แล้วคิดภาษีแบบขั้นบันได 0% / 5% / 10% / 15% / 20% / 25% / 30% / 35%

A big bonus makes your tax jump

Say your salary is ฿50,000 a month and you receive a ฿150,000 bonus.

Tax for the year goes from about ฿20,450 to ฿39,425+ almost ฿19,000

The catch: this extra tax is usually taken in the month you get the bonus, or you pay it when you file your return early next year. If you haven't planned for it, part of the bonus you meant to spend or save is gone.

Plan your tax for the whole year, not just at filing time

Three things are worth knowing at the start of the year:

  • Your take-home pay each month, as the base for planning your spending
  • Your tax for the year, including any bonus you expect
  • How much to put into tax-saving funds such as RMF or ThaiESG, and whether you'll still have enough to live on
